AI and Me

July 12, 2026 3 min read

It’s been some time since I started using AI for coding, and things have definitely changed.

Previously, I would go through blog posts, documentation, Stack Overflow, and countless Google searches to figure out why something was happening.

Then I got used to ChatGPT. Initially, it felt like a glorified Google search. Instead of sifting through multiple URLs to find what I needed, I could ask ChatGPT directly. Of course, how useful its answer was depended on how accurately I described what I was looking for.

For a while, everything was fine. Coding wasn’t dead; problem-solving still exists. I was patient enough to follow what ChatGPT was telling me.

But over time, I noticed I wasn’t reading as much. I was encouraged to be fast, and slowly lost the ability to sit with something—to read it and understand it. I began skipping most of the explanation and jumping straight to the code block that promised to fix the bug.

Then came Cursor. Now, I don’t always have to read the code either. A vague description of what I intend to do can generate a large amount of code that appears to do the task. Whether it does exactly what I wanted often feels secondary. If it works on first inspection, it’s easy to move on.

I don’t enjoy coding in the same way now. As someone once said:

“Manassil kuttabodham thonniyal pinne cheyyunnathellam yanthrikamai thonnum.”

When guilt enters the mind, everything that follows can start to feel mechanical.

I know I have to figure something out. My concern isn’t only what AI will do to my job. It’s whether this is how I want to spend nine hours of my day.

Right now, what I feel is confusion. But figuring things out is still what I intend to do.
